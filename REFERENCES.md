## What's an assumption, not a citation

Not everything in the two constants files is literature-backed — some numbers had no available source and are explicitly marked `[ASSUMPTION]` in the code, calibrated to produce physiologically plausible behavior rather than measured from data. The two most important ones to be upfront about in a defense:

- **Norepinephrine's EC50/Emax/Hill coefficient** (constants.py) — no published Emax model fit was found for this specific relationship; the values were back-calculated from steady-state plasma concentration ranges implied by real dosing data, not fitted to real MAP-response measurements.
- **Nitroprusside's EC50/Emax/Hill coefficient** (hypertensive_constants.py) — same situation, same reasoning. Sodium nitroprusside is dosed by direct hemodynamic titration in practice, not by target plasma level, so this kind of PK/PD curve fit is simply not something the clinical literature needed to establish.
- **All SVR-by-severity-profile bands for hypertensive emergency** — unlike septic shock (which has real invasive hemodynamic profiling studies, sources #5-6), hypertensive emergency patients are rarely instrumented with the catheters that measure SVR directly, so no equivalent literature exists. These bands are proportional escalations above normal, not measured data.

Being honest about which numbers are cited vs. assumed is a strength in an engineering/clinical-modeling defense, not a weakness — it shows you understand the difference between simulation and clinical fact.
